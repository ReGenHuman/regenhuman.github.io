ReGenHuman: Re-Generating Human Appearances for Realistic Full-Body Video Anonymization

Anonymous Authors
NeurIPS 2026 Submission
Paper arXiv Code

Past full-body anonymization methods either produce anatomically unrealistic outputs or fail to maintain consistent identity across frames. ReGenHuman regenerates humans from identity-free structural cues, achieving realistic, temporally consistent anonymization that preserves downstream utility.

Abstract

Anonymizing human-centric video data is an understudied problem. Prior anonymization techniques either blur or redact pixels at the cost of realism and downstream utility, or generate frame-by-frame at the cost of temporal coherence. We introduce ReGenHuman, the first full-body video anonymization pipeline that is simultaneously realistic, temporally consistent, and anonymous by construction.

Contrary to past approaches which redact or edit the inputs directly, we propose a regenerate, don't edit paradigm. Our approach composites 2D pose, segmentation, and monocular depth into two complementary conditioning streams—StructAll and StructHuman—which are used to fine-tune a video-to-video diffusion backbone on in-the-wild human videos, synthesizing the human regions entirely from identity-free structural cues. We evaluate our model on privacy, quality, and utility, and show that ReGenHuman achieves the best tradeoff across all three axes against current baselines. We further show that our anonymized videos remain effective for downstream tasks, including video question answering.

A Good Anonymizer Must Satisfy Three Axes

🔒

Privacy

The output must not be re-identifiable by any means—including through face, body, clothing, and background cues.

🧠

Utility

Actions, poses, human–object interactions, and scene context must remain recognizable for downstream tasks.

🎬

Quality

The video must be temporally and geometrically consistent and realistic enough for downstream model training.

Method

ReGenHuman replaces the prevailing edit-or-redact paradigm with a regenerate, don't edit formulation. The human region is entirely resynthesized from scratch, conditioned only on identity-free structural signals, so no original human pixel can reach the output.

The pipeline has three steps:

  1. Conditioning extraction. Per-frame human masks (SAM3), 2D pose skeletons (DWPose), monocular depth (Video Depth Anything), and a clip-level text caption are extracted from the input video using frozen, off-the-shelf models.
  2. Structural conditioning. The extracted signals are composited into one of two identity-free conditioning videos:
    • StructAll—uses depth as the background, yielding a fully anonymized frame with no original pixels anywhere. Suitable when background context must also be protected.
    • StructHuman—keeps the original background while graying out human regions, trading whole-frame anonymization for richer scene context. Suitable for settings where the background is not private (e.g., public streets).
  3. Diffusion-based regeneration. A rank-32 LoRA trained on Wan2.1-VACE is fine-tuned on in-the-wild human videos to invert the structural conditioning back into a photorealistic clip. The DiT backbone and pretrained Context Adapter weights remain frozen.

Because human-region pixels in the conditioning depend only on pose—never on the original appearance—privacy is enforced architecturally rather than empirically.

Contributions

First realistic, temporally consistent full-body video anonymization pipeline.

By jointly denoising full clips conditioned on structural overlays, ReGenHuman produces anonymized video that is temporally stable, anatomically coherent, and visually realistic.

A new paradigm: Regenerate, Don't Edit.

Unlike all prior methods that edit, redact, or perturb original pixels, we regenerate the human region from identity-free structural inputs (StructAll and StructHuman). This carries an architectural privacy guarantee: no original human pixel can reach the output.

A unified three-axis evaluation protocol.

We propose a comprehensive benchmark jointly measuring Privacy (identity, attribute, background-text leakage, adversarial body re-identification), Utility (action recognition, captioning, HOI preservation), and Quality (VBench- and VBench-2.0-aligned temporal consistency, human anatomy, and aesthetics).
